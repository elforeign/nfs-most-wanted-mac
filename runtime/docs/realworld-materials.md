# Physical car materials

- **Physical car materials** (`realworld_materials.c`; F10 Picture; `NFS_REALWORLD_MATERIALS=off|glass|chrome|all`): default **Off**, including all look presets. My picks saves the selection. Live variants affect only car-effect WINDSHIELD/WINDOWMASK (Glass) and CHROME/MAGCHROME/USER_RIMS_CHROME (Chrome). Other windows, lamps, plastics, painted wheels, brakes and exhaust retain their existing shading. Chrome draws whose diffuse is a lamp-state texture also keep the retail shader: stock lamp units draw CHROME with the per-car headlight/brakelight texture (`<base>_OFF/_ON/_DAMAGE0`, recorded by the GetUsedCarTextureInfo hook 00751860; headlights show `_ON`), and the retail CHROME record's DiffuseMin = DiffuseMax = 1 makes that texture the lamp's look, which zero-diffuse conductor shading would discard (grey/dark units). That is 10,199 LOD A triangles in 27 stock cars; `realworld_lamp_chrome_test.py` routes 0 of them physical (pre-fix 12,021 lamp-state draws) and keeps all 61,411 other chrome triangles physical. Off uses the original production shader sources, with seven frozen SHA-256 identities in `realworld_materials_test.py`; the selected path has separate cache keys, paired vertex layouts and per-pixel reflection vectors in the existing cube coordinate system.
  Glass keeps exact unpolarized dielectric Fresnel and incoherent internal reflections through the existing 4 mm clear pane. At 550 nm, n=1.525139, k=2.2e-7 and surface F0=.0432492 come from [Rubin's measured clear soda-lime glass constants](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polyanskiy/refractiveindex.info-database/main/database/data/glass/misc/soda-lime/nk/Rubin-clear.yml). An additional neutral Beer–Lambert absorbing layer sets whole-pane normal visible-light transmittance (VLT) to **.75 windscreen, .20 rear hatch/rear glass, .70 lateral glass, .10 roof glass**. The .75 windscreen target is a representative laminated-glazing proxy: the [UNECE glazing comparison, light-transmission row](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/617327/files/%5BE_%5DECE_TRANS_WP.29_2008_47-EN.pdf) records 75% for European windscreens and 70% for US/Japanese glazing; it is a reference threshold, not a measurement of this vehicle. [GM bulletin PIT3237F, page 2](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2012/SB-10073947-0699.pdf) specifies at least 70% for windscreen/front doors and nominal 20% for optional rear privacy glass. [AGC's factory privacy-glass data](https://www.agc-automotive.com/products-and-solutions/privacy-glass) gives a 4.5–40% product range and attributes absorption to altered glass composition. The .20 rear target is within the requested .20–.35 range; this selection is an artistic application of documented automotive values, not a claim that a particular Corvette shipped with privacy glass. [NHTSA's FMVSS 205 interpretation](https://www.nhtsa.gov/interpretations/aiam5441) explains the US 70% minimum for areas requisite for driving visibility, including all passenger-car windows; this renderer does not model certification or market-specific equipment.

  The material hook `006C76E0(eLightMaterial *, flags)` receives no mesh/part identifier. Both WINDSHIELD and WINDOWMASK span multiple panes: Corvette, GTI and COPSPORT combine disconnected panes in one draw/material section. The renderer therefore classifies **connected components within each indexed triangle-list material section**, welding identical object-space positions across normal/UV seams. It sums each triangle's area times the mean of its three authored vertex normals. Each component gets one VLT, carried in a separate vertex buffer and then texcoord4.w; normals used for Fresnel/reflection remain untouched. Every vertex of a component carries the same VLT, so interpolation and interior-view normal flips cannot produce tint patches. The two materials are classified independently; all corresponding clear/mask sections in the fixtures agree.

  In the car frame, +X is front, −X rear, Y lateral and +Z up. Pane direction uses triangle geometry rather than authored normals: compute each triangle cross product, flip it when its dot product with the triangle centroid is negative (away from the car-local origin), then sum the oriented cross products over the welded connected pane. Cross magnitude supplies area weighting. This removes both winding and normal reversals in lower LODs; geometry also preserves the Murcielago C deck class despite its changed authored normal. For the resulting direction `n`, **n.z/length(n) ≥ .98 selects roof**; otherwise **abs(n.x)/(abs(n.x)+abs(n.y)) ≤ .25 selects side**, with remaining positive/negative X selecting front/rear. The same thresholds give identical pane classes in LOD A–D. Degenerate components use the material fallback (.70 WINDOWMASK, .75 WINDSHIELD). This assumes car-local origin inside/below the glazing and outward-facing car panels; concave or offset-origin meshes can require a different rule. Nearly horizontal engine-deck glazing uses the roof class. Unsupported draw/layout types retain native rendering.

  The roof proxy is **10% normal VLT**, selected within [AGC's factory privacy-glass range of 4.5–40% (ISO 9050)](https://www.agc-automotive.com/products-and-solutions/privacy-glass), whose documented vehicle positions explicitly include **Roof**. It is a representative factory-tinted roof value, not a measured Brera specification. The same neutral absorption model applies; roof alpha at full coverage is .90. Default remains Off.

  The unshadowed **t0** technique binds **cube s0 / diffuse s1**, while shadowed t1–t5 bind **diffuse s0 / cube s1**. Physical glass and chrome preserve those native texture and sampler registers in their generated Metal declarations. The preceding generator accepted t0 but reused the shadowed resource declarations; the renderer still bound resources using the original retail reflection, leaving incompatible texture types at both slots. The regression finds **8 original texture/sampler binding mismatches → 0**, and compiled Metal reflection rejects **2 wrong texture types per original t0 variant → 0** before issuing a draw. GPU fixtures bind resources according to the retail technique and verify tint/reflection output for both t0 materials as well as all shadowed variants. This also corrects the shared unshadowed chrome resource path. Off retains all seven frozen source identities. Showroom appearance still needs an in-game check.

  Pane streams are built lazily on the first selected glass draw for a vertex/index buffer pair, layout, material fallback and draw range. Repeated draws perform one dictionary lookup using fixed-size metadata (buffer identities and write generations, offsets/stride, base vertex, index range/width and fallback); they never scan indices or hash vertex contents. Distinct ranges are necessary because shared buffers can contain several material sections. Writable D3D Lock and Unlock bump buffer generations and discard dependent streams; read-only locks preserve them. Buffer release also discards dependencies before identities can be reused. Entries live with their source buffers, with no 64-entry eviction/reclassification cycle; writes discard old generations rather than accumulating them. Off never enters this lookup/build path. The production-helper test shows **1 geometry scan/classification over 1,000 draws**, versus **1,000 scans** for the frozen preceding helper (which already cached the classifier itself). It checks both buffer write hooks, read-only locks, changed topology, 97 simultaneous entries, release/reuse and invalidation failure controls. Static draws use Metal's vertex ID including base vertex; copied draws use rebased indices and a zero stream base. Both production vertex layouts execute 24 GPU offset cases with zero errors; removing stream-base subtraction fails eight cases. A welded-seam fixture rejects equal face weights (six wrong vertices).

  With clear-pane transmission Tclear and refracted cosine ct, the effective layer contributes `exp(log(VLT/Tclear(1))/ct)`. Reflection is unchanged; the layer only attenuates transmission and grows darker with optical path length. This is a neutral visible-light approximation with a clear-pane Fresnel front surface, not spectral integration or a multilayer laminate solver. Texture alpha supplies coverage independently: source alpha is coverage × (1−T), source RGB is coverage × R × environment / source alpha, so ordinary alpha blending yields coverage × R reflection plus (1−coverage+coverage × T) destination transmission. No second material-alpha reflection factor or legacy reflection boost applies. The retail path and default Off selection retain their existing shader identities. Windows currently has no physical-material HLSL counterpart (`runtime/win/image/hlsl.h`, `host/shaders/sm_hlsl.cpp`); this optional Metal path does not add one.
  Chrome uses exact unpolarized conductor Fresnel with n=3.1812121212, k=3.3290909091 (linear interpolation at 550 nm), F0=.554536, from [Johnson–Christy's measured chromium constants](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/polyanskiy/refractiveindex.info-database/master/database/data/main/Cr/nk/Johnson.yml). Direct lighting uses isotropic GGX alpha=.006 and Smith masking, with zero diffuse and independent of legacy zero specular masks. The roughness is the polished chrome sample fit in [Burley, Figure 9](https://cgvr.cs.uni-bremen.de/teaching/cg_literatur/Physically-Based%20Shading%20at%20Disney%20-%20Ken%20Burley%2C%202012%2C%20v3.pdf). Environment integration uses 16 deterministic GGX importance samples at a footprint-aware cube level (as the retail implicit LOD) over the capture mips, weighted by the retail vertex occlusion saturate(2 × colour.x) (PC car data stores unoccluded as 0.5; the raw value halved body-chrome reflections on 51,285 of 72,262 stock chrome vertices); existing cube mips are not treated as a GGX convolution. This first slice is a neutral 550 nm proxy, not spectrally integrated RGB or a claim about the chemistry of every mesh named CHROME. Existing cube resolution and 16 samples limit narrow-highlight fidelity. No external code was copied.
