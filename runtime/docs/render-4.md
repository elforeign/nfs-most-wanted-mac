- **Bloom on bright areas** (`NFS_BLOOM_SURFACES=glow|halo|natural`, F10 Graphics > Bloom on bright areas, default `natural` (`settings_port.c:106`                                                                                              ): `natural` conserves energy: scene + 0.20 x (blur(B) - B) at Bloom 100 %, spreading near-white light without lifting a broad bright surface or adding a ring (`x360_post.m:212–217`). The earlier Glow/Halo comparison follows: the maskless bright pass lets any surface above the knee bloom, and the bloom chain lifts an area bigger than its kernel by ~6.2x its bright-pass excess (5x5 gauss and downsample sum 1, the Bloom kernels ~1.96 each, x bloom scale 1.6), so sun-lit snow caps, smoke/spray and lit road strips clip flat, more so as the adapted luminance drops. `halo` subtracts that own lift (the 5x5-blurred bright pass x the kernels' gain, `x_bloom_gain` in `render_post.m`) in the composite, so such areas keep their texture and only the light spilling past their edges is added. - **Wet road reflection** (`roadfilm.m`; F10 Picture): Retail is the default. Water film (`NFS_ROAD_FILM=1`) multiplies only the existing wet reflection RGB by exact unpolarized air/water Fresnel (index 1.33, normal-incidence reflectance 0.020059). It uses the geometric normal and the existing tangent-space eye vector (t4); diffuse, Phong specular, coverage, wetness, fog and ripple controls retain their equations. Both choices apply live through separate shader variants; Retail retains the original shader source byte for byte. Zero-wetness draws select Retail even with Water film enabled. This is an angular Fresnel experiment, without calibrated wet roughness or an energy-partition model. Diagnostics: held `BOOT_DIAG_LOOK_AB=filmoff,filmon` pairs at the same sim tick and exposure.
- Headlight projection (`headlights.m` / `headlight_profile.c`, hooks in `mirror.c` StuffScenery and `render.m`): **On by default**. `NFS_HEADLIGHT_PROJECTION=0|off` disables it at startup. The player's two low beams illuminate opaque world, road and car surfaces in the main view, with the R112 upper envelope feathered inward over 0.2° and the -5° near boundary over 0.5°, inverse square, receiver cosine, 100–120 m fade and two 1024-square lamp depth maps re-culled from resident scenery and cars. No volumetric beam is added. Carbon retains its native headlights.

  The photometric grid remains in candela. Its original conversion, diffuse reflectance × lux / (π × 100), was too small for the authored night scene: the post chain bottoms out at adapted luminance .06 (maximum ordinary exposure 4.590), rather than continuing to expose the dark road. A dark asphalt receiver (1% linear reflectance) .65 m below the lamp at 50 m received only .00000201992 renderer radiance. The clock now maps the lamps into the authored night units with `1 + 8191 × night`; midnight effective units are 100/8192 cd/m² per renderer unit, giving .0165472 radiance on that receiver. This is a visibility calibration for the game's authored radiance, not a brighter physical bulb or a claim of calibrated display luminance. The candela grid, aim, inverse square, shadows, albedo decoding and fog retain their equations; only the renderer’s angular boundary is feathered inward, without adding upward light. Dusk blends smoothly; the existing lamp level is zero from 07:00 to 17:00, so noon prepares no maps or projected draws. With the clock Off the original physical conversion remains.

  Caster and receiver transforms subtract the common enabled-lamp origin in double precision before packing the float vertex matrix. Interpolating kilometre-scale absolute world positions previously corrupted the receiver-plane derivatives, causing speckled self-shadowing under perspective projection. Lamp-relative interpolation removes that loss; the existing four weighted depth comparisons, per-texel receiver-plane correction and 2e-7 depth tolerance remain. The regression renders a perspective wall as both caster and receiver: visibility standard deviation is 0/255 with mean 1, versus 68.590138/255 and mean .763532 with the pinned original packing. A separate 0.002° angular sweep has maximum grayscale edge step 3.429225/255 (limit 10), versus 149.631958/255 with the original cut-off. Both checks first failed on the original code; an independently compiled control restores the two SHA-256-pinned fragments and must fail again.

- **Clear coat** (`clearcoat.c`; F10 Cars > Clear coat): port extra, **On by default** (owner's eye); `NFS_CLEARCOAT=off|on` (unset or empty On). On uses separate car t0–t5 variants, including a paired VS/PS for t0; Off preserves the original source and cache key byte for byte. Shadowed t1–t5 views need X360 car paint. Native t0 retains its PC base and cube-s0/diffuse-s1 sampler order in mirrors, reflections and shadowless views. Only `REGPAINT*`, `METPAINT*` and `PEARL*` receive lacquer. Schlick Fresnel uses IOR 1.5 and F0 = 0.04, following [Burley, *Physically Based Shading at Disney*, sections 5.4–5.5](https://media.disneyanimation.com/uploads/production/publication_asset/48/asset/s2012_pbs_disney_brdf_notes_v3.pdf). A power-256 mirror lobe approximates smooth lacquer (about 4.2° half width), rather than a measured roughness or full microfacet BRDF. Both sun and cube use the smooth normal above the flake and normal map. The complete Off result `B` (diffuse, specular/hotspot, flake and original environment) passes through two interfaces: `T = (1−F(N·V))²`. Its ordinary **0.5× env** balance and normal-map **2× authored Fresnel** stay inside `B`. A separate smooth cube uses alpha/AO visibility without those base reflectance masks. Sun share `d = min(F(N·V), F(V·H)·lobe)·lit` and cube share `F−d` partition the coat reflection. Output is `T·B + min((F−d)·env + d·sun, max(limit−T·B, 0))`, with per-channel `limit = max(1, B, env, sun)`. Only the coat addition can be clamped; the base is never normalized. Unit-radiance inputs remain at most one; inherited HDR paint and HDR illumination survive within that input bound. Normalizing the base to one and replacing its environment previously erased bright highlights. At N·V = N·L = 0.7, N·H = 1, the unit-white ordinary fixture has Off diffuse/spec/hotspot/env = 1/1/5/0.5 (total 7.5): old On was 1, fixed On is 6.920781, with diffuse transmission 0.917126 and added coat 0.042333. These fixture normals are not reconstructed from a screen capture. Metal checks reject the shipped highlight loss, grazing darkening and incorrect diffuse attenuation; full-paint radiance bounds, smooth directional-cube reflections, flake, zero exponents, t0 HDR/gating/link and removed-lobe/attenuation/doubled-env controls also pass. Held `BOOT_DIAG_LOOK_AB=coatoff,coaton` pairs share scene and exposure; variants preload even when Off. Garage close-ups keep the base highlight (BMW M3 GTR, Porsche Carrera GT); solo 4K cost pending.
