# Game controller (part 2 of 2)

Part of the runtime documentation; the index and switch lookup hint are in `runtime/README.md`.

**Beyond the X360 (all on by default):** cobble, stone and railroad add a made-up road texture (light motor 8 -> 16 Hz, 0.12 -> 0.28 peak, heavy up to 0.06, 3 -> 40 m/s; concrete left out, it is ordinary road in much of the city), `NFS_RUMBLE_SURFACES=x360` / F10 Controls "Rumble on cobbles" restores the console                                                   . Prop hits (type-1 contacts with a non-vehicle) use the X360's world formula of the speed change, instead of its player-speed ramp that made a box at 50 m/s feel like a wall. Each prop hit has a minimum **0.25 strength for a 0.10 s fade**; strength and duration above those floors scale with the speed change as before                                                   . Tune `kPropHitMinStrength` and `kPropHitMinSeconds` in feedback.c independently. `NFS_RUMBLE_PROPS=x360` / "Prop hits rumble by impact" restores the console formula and envelope, without those floors. Vehicle hits keep the console strength but fade over **strength * 0.45 s** (full hit: 450 ms); while a collision envelope runs, an equal/weaker vehicle hit cannot prolong it, and only a hit stronger than its original peak restarts it. Tune `kVehicleHitFullFadeSeconds`; `NFS_RUMBLE_VEHICLES=x360` (environment only) restores the console's strength-second duration and fmax extension on repeated vehicle contacts. World and ground contacts retain the original behavior. Each switch affects its own contact/surface class; use all three `=x360` switches for the original console model.

Test switches: `NFS_TEST_SURFACE=cobble|stone|railroad|<hex key>` (the model reads that surface under every tyre; physics untouched), `NFS_FFB_AUTODRIVE=1` (the model also runs while the AI drives; add `BOOT_PAD=sim` since the model advances on pad reads). Verify with **`NFS_RUMBLE=off`**: `feedback: collision` lines include requested duration, actual envelope time before/after the contact, and `vehicle restart`, `vehicle held`, `prop`, or `x360`. A held vehicle hit must leave the remaining time unchanged; a tiny isolated prop hit reports strength 0.25, duration 0.100 s. `BOOT_DIAG` also writes `rumble.tsv` (motor levels, collision envelope, speed and each tyre's surface over time; every collision as a `#` line with type, dv, speed and strength) and `feedback: tyre ticks by surface` lines; `surfaces.txt` lists every curve entry.

A bare `BOOT_PAD=sim` means `sim:xbox` (never the real controller). `BOOT_PAD=sim:rt=1,lx=-0.5,buttons=1,...` simulates a pad with constant values for tests (`buttons` is the physical mask: bit 0 ✕/A, 1 ○/B, 2 □/X, 3 △/Y, 4 L1, 5 R1, 6 Create, 7 Options, 8 L3, 9 R3, 10 L2, 11 R2, 12 PS, 13 Touchpad, 14/15 Edge paddles; `lt`/`rt` are the analog triggers; a bare word is its identity: `dualsense`, `ds4`, `edge`, or `xbox`, the default, e.g. `BOOT_PAD=sim:dualsense`). A last `@T1-T2-T3...` connects the simulated pad T1 seconds after start-up, disconnects it at T2, connects it again at T3, ... (`BOOT_PAD=sim:dualsense,buttons=1@30-50-60`: hot-plug tests; without it the pad is there from start-up). `BOOT_KEYS` step `padtype:xbox` (or `dualsense`, ...) changes the simulated pad's type mid-run. `padlayout:xbox|playstation` changes only this run's layout (no app preference write; `padlayout:auto` goes back to the pick or the pad's own); `padtriggers:0.50/0.75:8` holds physical LT/RT together for 8 ticks, before layout/F10 neutralization, and clears both on release; with the simulated pad, as with a real one, L2/R2 read pressed whenever their value exceeds 0, and a PlayStation pad's scripted L2/R2 button bit is a full press. `padbindps` binds Accelerate / Brake to R2 / L2 on a PlayStation pad as a player's capture records them (Rz+ / Z+; the smoke's native trigger phase). A PlayStation pad's L2/R2 keep their pressure through any binding (an axis half: half travel = half demand); the PS2 defaults' R2/L2 shifts press at half travel (the game's digital mappings, `IO_UpdateFromDevice` 006448E0). With diagnostics, `padtriggers: sample` pairs the DI Z/Rz sample with the following poll's pre-Fetch GameDevice gas/brake values. With `BOOT_DIAG`, `events.log` records the controller found and every input change (`pad:` lines). Scripted trigger pairs also record `pedals.tsv`: human physics State gas/brake (`Chassis::ComputeState` 0069D4D0, +0x4C/+0x5C) and PInput values after `InputPlayer::FetchInput` 006A8870, including the game's endpoint snap, automatic reverse and automatic braking. `pedals_check.py <diag dir>` requires the 0/.25/.5/.75/1 sweeps, simultaneous .5/.75 and 1/1, and release above 8 m/s (at rest automatic braking can produce 1 with both pedals released).
